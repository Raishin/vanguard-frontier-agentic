# Safety checklist

Use this reference before any recommendation involving Microsoft license changes, spend, or business-value claims.

## Non-negotiables

- Never make or imply a licensing purchase commitment, contract term, discount, or guaranteed savings figure.
- Do not invent adoption percentages, usage counts, assisted-hours/value, or ROI numbers. Require evidence from admin center usage reports, Adoption Score, or Copilot Analytics.
- Note metric latency (up to ~72h) and any documented known-issue windows before quoting figures.
- Never ask users to paste credentials, tenant IDs, environment URLs, or customer data into chat.
- Use current official Microsoft Learn documentation for adoption measurement and Copilot reporting behavior; verify metric formulas are current.
- Tie every recommendation to a measurable indicator with a baseline, target, and kill criterion.

## Stress checks

- Which assigned licenses are inactive or under-utilized (waste)?
- Is the rollout instrumented with a baseline before claiming value?
- Are success criteria defined before deployment or retrofitted afterward?
- Is "ROI" derived from evidence, or asserted?
- Does a recommendation to buy more licenses precede reclaiming inactive ones?

## Evidence labels

Use `documented artifact`, `user-provided evidence`, `documentation-based`, or `inference`. Documentation alone never proves the user's actual license utilization, adoption score, productivity impact, or ROI.

## Decision boundaries (escalate / refuse)

- Refuse to produce a guaranteed savings or ROI figure; provide a measurable estimate method instead.
- Escalate licensing purchase, true-up, or contract decisions to procurement and the licensing owner.
- Do not recommend disabling security/governance controls to boost an adoption metric.
- Do not present adoption metrics in a way that identifies individuals below the minimum group-size privacy threshold.
